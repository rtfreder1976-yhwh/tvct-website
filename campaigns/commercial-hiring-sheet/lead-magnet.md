# The Hiring Sheet

## 12 questions to ask any commercial cleaning company — and the answers to get in writing

Most cleaning proposals read the same: insured, professional, attention to detail. None of that tells you who will be in your building at 9 PM or what happens when a restroom gets skipped. These twelve questions do. Ask them of every company you're considering — including us — and don't accept an answer that isn't written down.

## If you only ask three

1. **Question 3** — the insurance certificate, with the limit on it
2. **Question 4** — the task list, item by item
3. **Question 9** — the make-right policy, in writing

Those three eliminate most of the field before you ever compare prices.

---

## The people

**1. Who is actually doing the work, and how experienced are they?**
The name on the proposal doesn't clean anything. The person in your building does — and in this industry, crews change. What matters is whether the company knows exactly who is assigned to you and can vouch for them.
*Get in writing:* who is assigned to your building and how experienced they are.

**2. Is every person background-checked — or is that "available upon request"?**
Your building has keys, alarm codes, files, and after-hours access. "We can arrange checks" means they haven't been done.
*Get in writing:* confirmation that background checks are complete for everyone assigned to your building, before the first visit.

**3. What insurance is behind them, and for how much?**
"Fully insured" without a number is not information. If something is damaged, the limit on the certificate is what matters.
*Get in writing:* the certificate of insurance itself, with the liability limit printed on it.

## The work

**4. Is the scope a task list, or an adjective?**
"Thorough cleaning of all areas" is not a scope. A scope is a list of tasks — empty this, wipe that, vacuum here — with a frequency next to each one. If a company can't produce the list, they can't hold their own crew to it either.
*Get in writing:* the task list for your building, item by item.

**5. What happens on every visit, and what happens weekly or monthly?**
Trash and restrooms are not the same job as floors and vents. Vague scopes hide the difference until you notice something hasn't been done in a month.
*Get in writing:* the schedule showing which tasks happen on which visits.

**6. Who checks the work, and how often will you hear about it?**
If nobody inspects, you are the inspector. That's a job you're paying to not have.
*Get in writing:* how quality is checked, by whom, and how issues reach you.

## The price

**7. How is the price calculated?**
A defensible commercial price comes from three things: square footage, the task list, and visits per week. If the number can't be traced to those inputs, it was guessed — and guessed numbers change later.
*Get in writing:* a quote that shows its inputs.

**8. How long does a written quote take?**
A company that can't put a number in writing within a business day will not be faster when something actually matters.
*Get in writing:* a commitment in hours, not "we'll get back to you."

## When something goes wrong

**9. What is the make-right policy?**
Something will eventually get missed. The question is whether there's a defined window to report it and a defined response — or a negotiation.
*Get in writing:* the reporting window and exactly what happens next.

**10. What happens with your keys and alarm codes?**
Who holds them, where they're recorded, and what happens the day a cleaner leaves the company.
*Get in writing:* the access-control policy.

## The relationship

**11. Who do you actually call?**
A named person who answers, or an inbox. You'll find out which on the worst possible day, so find out now.
*Get in writing:* your point of contact and the response time you can expect.

**12. What does the agreement lock you into?**
Term length, cancellation notice, and how much warning you get before the price changes.
*Get in writing:* all three, before you sign.
