# VSL Script — "The Exact Price"

_Draft 1, 2026-08-25. Runtime ~3:40. Written to work three ways: Todd on camera, an unattributed narrator, or voiceover over b-roll. Bracketed **[IDENTITY]** lines are the only text that changes between versions._

**Reading note:** unhurried. One idea per sentence, a pause before every number. The tone is a knowledgeable friend explaining something over a kitchen counter — not a pitch. Nothing here should be read with urgency.

---

> **[IDENTITY — unattributed version]**

> We're not the cheapest cleaning company around here.
>

**On screen:** charcoal text, one phrase in peach — *That's intentional.*
**B-roll:** none yet. Hold on the speaker. Let the line land.

## Beat 2 — Why the price is the price (0:25-1:30)

> Here's how most cleaning companies quote a house. Somebody comes out, walks around, looks at your kitchen, and gives you a number. Or you get a "starting at" price that climbs by the time somebody's actually standing in your driveway.
>
> We don't do either one.
>
> Your price comes from two things: your square footage, and the type of cleaning you want. That's it. Not an hourly rate. Not a guess. Not what your house looks like on the day we happen to show up.
>
> So when you call, you tell us the square footage, and we read your price off a rate card. You'll have the number before you hang up.
>
> And that number is the number on your bill.
>
> It moves in exactly two situations, and both of them are yours: you add something to the job, or the details we quoted from need correcting — square footage, bedrooms, baths, pets. Either way, you hear from us before anything changes. Not after.

**On screen:** *Square footage + type of cleaning. That's the whole formula.*
**B-roll:** a rate card on a desk; a phone screen; someone writing a number down. Never a cleaner "inspecting" a house — we don't do that.

---

## Beat 3 — What the number buys (1:30-2:40)

> So what are you actually paying for?
>
> Every regular visit runs a written 44-item checklist. The same 44 items, in the same order, every single time. That's not a slogan — it's the reason your house looks the same after the twentieth visit as it did after the first. The clean doesn't depend on who's on the crew that day, or what they happened to notice.
>
> Every cleaner is background-checked before they ever walk into a home. The company carries two million dollars in liability insurance — that's double what most cleaning companies carry, and we'll email you the certificate before your first clean if you want to see it.
>
> And if we missed something, you tell us within twenty-four hours and we'll get a re-clean on the schedule right away. No charge, no argument.
>
> Regular cleaning starts at two hundred dollars. If you put it on a schedule, that comes down — thirty percent off for weekly, twenty-five for every two weeks, fifteen for monthly.

**On screen, one at a time:** *44 items, every visit* · *Background-checked* · *$2,000,000 liability* · *Re-clean within 24 hours*
**B-roll:** an actual checklist page; one specific detail being cleaned — a ceiling fan blade, the top of a door frame, a baseboard edge. Specificity is the whole visual rule.

---

## Beat 4 — Who we're not for (2:40-3:10)

> I'll save you some time, though.
>
> We'd love to clean for everybody. We can't.
>
> If you need somebody there today, that's not us — we usually book two or three days out.
>
> And if you want a cleaner who'll do it exactly the way you tell them to, room by room, your list instead of ours — honestly, that's not us either. We run a system. That's the whole product.
>
> If either of those is what you're after, there are good companies around here that work that way, and I'd rather you find one than be disappointed in us.

**On screen:** nothing. This beat is the speaker's face, or plain ground. Let it be sincere.

---

## Beat 5 — The ask (3:10-3:40)

> But if you want a company that shows up when it said it would, works the same checklist every time, and tells you the truth about money —
>
> Call us. Tell us your square footage and what kind of cleaning you're after, and you'll have your exact price before you hang up.
>
> No walkthrough. No estimate that changes. Just the number.

## VCT Voice Test (10 questions, `brand/voice-profile.md`)

1. **Sounds like a real person?** Yes — contractions, asides, one-idea sentences.
2. **Demonstrates expertise or claims it?** Demonstrates: the formula, the checklist order, the two exceptions. The word "expert" never appears.
3. **Value communicated without apologizing for price?** Yes — "That's intentional," stated flat, explained immediately.
4. **Humor subtle?** Barely present by design. "I'll save you some time, though" is the only dry turn. Correct for a money video.
5. **Customer the butt of a joke?** No.
6. **Sounds like a generic cleaning company?** No — no sparkle, no "sit back and relax," no stock benefit language.
7. **Sounds like a marketing agency wrote it?** No — no big-promise open, no urgency, no stacked offer.
8. **Would we say this to a customer in their kitchen?** Yes, nearly verbatim. That was the drafting test.
9. **"These people know what they're doing"?** Yes — the 44-item order and the two exceptions do that work.
10. **"I like these people"?** Beat 4 is the line that earns it: telling someone not to hire you is the most likable thing in the script.

**Banned-word check:** no best / premium / luxury / exceptional / superior / unmatched / unparalleled / elite / five-star. No spotless, sparkling, pristine. No free quote, no same-day, no bonded, no workers' comp, no employee-vs-contractor framing. No exclamation marks. All numbers from `claims.ts`.
