Proposed titleThe Story Behind The Valley Clean Team: How a Broken Leg and a Layoff Built a Family-Owned Cleaning Company
Meta descriptionThe founding story of The Valley Clean Team: veteran-owned, family-run, started on a couch with under $5,000. How Todd and Christen Frederickson built it.
URL slugthe-story-behind-the-valley-clean-team

Article body

Most companies get a tidy origin story after the fact. The Valley Clean Team got its start the way real life tends to happen: all at once, at the worst possible time, on a couch.

Co-founders Todd and Christen Frederickson recently sat down with the Koalified Podcast to talk about how they built the company. What comes through is not a highlight reel. It is an honest account of a family that got knocked down, decided to build something anyway, and chose to do it with more care for people than most of the industry bothers with.

A hard season that changed everything

Both Todd and Christen are Alabama-born. Christen grew up in the Muscle Shoals and Tuscumbia area; Todd is from Decatur, where his dad worked for the Tennessee Valley Authority. They had lived all over the country, from Atlanta to Las Vegas to Washington state, before coming back to North Alabama for the small-town feel and the central location.

Then came a stretch in 2021 and 2022 when everything arrived at once. Their daughter Charlotte was born in late 2021, and not long after, Todd's father passed away. Todd shattered his leg badly enough to be airlifted, and spent five months unable to put weight on it. That October, Christen was laid off from her credit-union marketing job, walking away with a severance and a ten-month-old at home.

It was, by any measure, the wrong time to start a business.

There is no perfect time to start. We were literally forced to start.

That is the part they keep coming back to. The couch Todd was stuck on turned out to be where the idea took shape. Immobile and restless, he started watching videos about buying and building small businesses â€” Cody Sanchez's, in particular â€” and the two of them brainstormed the company name in a doctor's office waiting room. Looking back, Christen sees the whole stretch differently now.

God put you on that couch, because we just would not have done it.

Built with the internet, their brains, and time

The Valley Clean Team launched in November 2022 and booked its first customer that December. The startup budget was under $5,000, and there was no bank loan behind it.

We had the internet, our brains, and time.

That is genuinely how it began. What made it workable was a support network close by: family land next door, and two people willing to do the unglamorous work themselves. Todd and Christen do not sit above the business. They run the office, and they still do the cleaning too.

We don't just run the business. We actually do the work too.

Neither of them came from a family that pushed entrepreneurship. The advice they grew up with was the opposite: if you can work for someone else, do it. But the pieces were there quietly all along. Todd's grandfather ran a tire store that has carried the Frederickson name since 1946, and Christen's mother was a serial entrepreneur and tailor. When the safety of a steady paycheck disappeared, building something of their own stopped feeling like a gamble and started feeling like the only honest option left.

You can read more about who they are and why they do this on the about The Valley Clean Team page.

A different deal for the people who do the work

The clearest sign of what kind of company this is comes from how they treat their cleaners. Most of the cleaning industry pays providers a small cut of each job. The Valley Clean Team runs on a 50/50 split, and the founders are deliberate about the language they use for it.

They don't work for us. We work with them.

Providers keep control of their own schedules and which jobs they take. The company handles everything else: marketing, client management, scheduling, the occasional hard conversation, and getting people paid. The 50/50 split is a meaningful change in how that work is valued.

Todd's rule of thumb for who they bring on is not years of experience or speed. It is heart. He tells the story of a provider cleaning for a man with dementia who came away saying her heart was full for having helped him. That is the person they want on a job. It is also, not coincidentally, a page from the playbook Todd admires most: hire people who are smarter than you, then give them room to do their best work.

Quality over quantity, and honest about mistakes

Ask the founders what they are building toward and they will not say the biggest cleaning company in the state. They will say the most trusted one.

We'd rather have quality, not quantity. Thirty five-star reviews than a hundred one-star reviews.

That standard shows up in small, steady ways. In one two-week stretch their Google reviews grew from 28 to 36, a pace Todd describes simply as 1% better every day. They are also plain about the fact that no cleaning service is perfect, which is why they stand behind their work with a guarantee. If something is not right, they make it right.

For a family with a new baby, boundaries became part of the model too. The office keeps 8-to-5 hours with a hard stop at five for family time, a discipline they learned the hard way after the accident. As Todd puts it, money cannot buy happiness, but it can buy time.

Rooted in North Alabama and Middle Tennessee

The Valley Clean Team now serves a growing footprint across two states: house cleaning in Huntsville, plus Athens, Florence and the Shoals, Mountain Brook and greater Birmingham, and Nashville. The name is a nod to the Tennessee Valley the founders came home to, and the company stays close to its neighbors through local networking, Facebook groups, and community giving. Their Veterans Day giveaway has been one of the more meaningful things they do, fitting for a veteran-owned business whose roots run deep.

Faith and gratitude sit underneath all of it. Todd and Christen are open Christians, and they have been candid about the harder parts of their story too, including Christen's honesty about her mental-health struggles and the loss of Todd's father, a Vietnam veteran, just after Charlotte was born. Gratitude, they say, is something you cultivate on purpose, every day.

What it means for you as a customer

When you hire The Valley Clean Team, you are hiring a company that knows what a hard season feels like and treats the people it works with like partners. That care tends to reach the customer's front door. Whether you need a standard clean before company arrives, a deep clean to reset a whole house, or a move-in / move-out cleaning to hand back a clean place, the same values are behind every visit: honesty, quality, and a team that actually cares.

Life is messy. That is the whole reason this company exists.

Body

Quick honesty up front: my wife and I run a cleaning company in North Alabama. I'm not selling anything here and I won't link it â€” I got a lot out of posts like this when we were figuring it out, so I'm paying it back with the specifics people usually leave out.

Context, because it matters for the "I don't have money/time" crowd: we started in late 2022 with a 10-month-old, me five months into a leg I couldn't walk on, and my wife freshly laid off. That was the starting line, not an excuse to wait. First year is tracking around $250K. Here's what actually got us there.

1. The under-$5K breakdown
We did not take a loan. The real costs early on were small and mostly software + insurance + supplies + a bit of paid lead flow. What we spent money on vs. didn't:
- Spent on: business formation, liability insurance, a scheduling/booking platform, basic cleaning supplies, and a small amount of paid leads to prime the pump.
- Did NOT spend on: office space, employees on payroll, vehicles, branding agencies, a fancy website on day one.
The mindset that kept the number low: "we had the internet, our brains, and time." If a problem could be solved by learning instead of buying, we learned it.

2. The pay model (this is the real unlock)
Most cleaning companies keep ~80% of the ticket and pay the cleaner ~20%. We flipped it closer to a 50/50 split with our providers.
Sounds insane until you run it out: fairly paid people do careful work, careful work earns 5-star reviews, reviews bring the next client at $0 acquisition cost. We treat providers as partners, not labor â€” they keep control of their own schedule and which jobs they take. We handle the parts they hate: marketing, scheduling, client management, collecting payment, and the awkward conversations.
If you run any labor-based service business, this is the lever. Underpaying your people is the most expensive thing you can do.

3. Booking software did the heavy lifting
We run more than half our daily operations through a booking platform (we use BookingKoala; there are others). Online booking, provider app, reporting, and referral tracking meant two people could run the volume of a much bigger shop. Automate scheduling and payment before you hire an admin â€” the software is cheaper than a person and doesn't call in sick.

4. The moat nobody else bothered with
We joined our local Realtor Association as an affiliate member. That gives us access to lockboxes for move-in/move-out cleans that most cleaning companies simply can't get into. It's a small annual cost and almost no competitor does it. Look for the boring credential in your industry that unlocks a whole client type â€” it's usually sitting there for a small fee that nobody bothers to pay.

5. Marketing was almost entirely organic
No big ad budget. What worked, roughly in order: local Facebook groups and mom's groups, BNI-style networking, Google Local Services, plus Thumbtack/Yelp to fill gaps. One partner is the extrovert who networks; the other works the online communities. A Veterans Day giveaway was our single most effective campaign. Pick the two channels that fit your personality and go deep instead of spreading thin.

6. Boundaries are a business strategy, not a soft skill
The thing I underestimated: we work 8â€“5 with a hard stop, then it's family time. After the baby and the accident we learned the hard way that "money can't buy happiness, but it can buy you time" â€” and that you protect the time on purpose or the business eats it. Willpower feels infinite. Time is not. Burned-out owners make bad decisions and lose their good people.

A few blunt lessons
- There is no perfect time to start. We were forced to start and it was the best thing that happened to us.
- Good enough beats nothing. You build the airplane while you fly it.
- Quality over quantity â€” 30 five-star reviews beat 100 one-star reviews.
- Hire for heart. Skills you can teach; caring about a stranger's home you can't.
