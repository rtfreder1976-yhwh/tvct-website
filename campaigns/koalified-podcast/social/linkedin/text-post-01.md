There is no perfect time to start a business.

We know, because we were forced to.

In 2022 we had a 10-month-old, I was five months into a shattered leg I couldn't put weight on, and my wife Christen had just been laid off from her marketing job — a surprise "retirement" with a severance and a baby at home.

That's when we started The Valley Clean Team.

Not from an office. From a couch. I was immobile, so I did the only thing I could: I read, I watched, I learned how service businesses actually work.

We launched in November 2022. First customer in December. Total spent to get going: under $5,000. No bank loan.

The way Christen puts it — "we had the internet, our brains, and time."

What I'd tell anyone waiting for the right moment:

- Good enough is better than nothing. You build the airplane while you fly it.
- Willpower can feel infinite. Time is not.
- The couch you're stuck on might be the reason it finally happens. We'd have talked ourselves out of it if life hadn't sat us down.

First year is on track for around $250,000. Still the two of us doing the work, plus the team we've built.

We didn't start because it was smart timing. We started because we ran out of reasons to wait.
