**1/**
In 2022 we had a 10-month-old, a shattered leg, and a surprise layoff — all at once.

That's the year we started a cleaning company for under $5,000.

On track for ~$250K in year one. Here's how. 🧵

---

**2/**
The setup, honestly, was terrible.

New baby (Charlotte). I couldn't put weight on my leg for 5 months. Then Christen got "unceremoniously laid off" from her marketing job — a surprise retirement with a severance.

We didn't pick the timing. It picked us.

---

**3/**
There is no perfect time to start.

We were literally forced to start. I was stuck on a couch, immobile, so I did the one thing I could: learn.

Watched, read, and figured out how service businesses actually make money.

---

**4/**
Total cost to launch: under $5,000. No bank loan.

Christen's line: "We had the internet, our brains, and time."

The support that mattered wasn't capital. It was family land next door and a reason to not quit.

---

**5/**
Launched November 2022. First customer December 2022.

We didn't wait to feel ready. Good enough is better than nothing. You build the airplane while you fly it.

---

**6/**
The model is where we broke from the industry.

Cleaning companies usually keep ~80% of the ticket. The cleaner keeps ~20%.

We split 50/50.

---

**7/**
"They don't work for us — we work with them."

Providers keep their own schedule and choose their jobs. We handle the boring stuff: marketing, scheduling, client management, payment, hard conversations.

---

**8/**
Why give away half?

Because we hire for heart. One provider cleaned for a man with dementia and told us, "my heart is full for helping this man."

You don't keep that person on 20%.

---

**9/**
We chose quality over quantity from day one.

30 five-star reviews beat 100 one-star reviews. Fairly paid providers do careful work. Careful work is the only marketing that compounds.

---

**10/**
Growth has been boring and steady. 1% better every day.

Google reviews went 28 → 36 in about two weeks. Willpower can feel infinite; time isn't. So we protect the time.

---

**11/**
If you're waiting for the right moment: it isn't coming.

We started broke, hurt, and unemployed. It worked because we started.
