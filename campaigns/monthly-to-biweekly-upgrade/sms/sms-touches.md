# SMS — Monthly → Biweekly Upgrade

_Created 2026-08-25. Status: DRAFT. GHL SMS confirmed live by Todd 2026-08-25._

## Why there is only one

These are **11 existing customers** who already pay us and have had us in their homes. The relationship is worth more than the upgrade. Two texts about spending more money would damage something that currently works, for a 15%-to-25% delta.

One text, and only after the emails have run. If they ignore all three emails, they have answered.

## Rules

- Under 300 characters. First name. Signed **Todd**. Warmest register.
- **Never implies their house is dirty.** Same rule as the emails — this is the easiest place in the system to insult a customer by accident.
- No exclamation marks (this message contains a number, and the rule bans pairing them).
- AL: (256) 826-1100. TN customers: (615) 510-1427.
- Business hours, and **not on their service day** — a text about spending more on the day we are in their house reads badly.

## The touch

### SMS 1 — Day 14 (two days after email 3, the easy-no)

**Send:** Day 14, Tue-Thu, 5:30 PM CT. Only to contacts who did not reply to any of the three emails.

> Hi {{contact.first_name}} — Todd with The Valley Clean Team. Last thing on the every-two-weeks option: it is 25% off instead of 15%, so the per-visit price drops. Happy to run your number, or happy to leave your schedule exactly as it is. — Todd

**245 characters.** Purpose: one plain restatement of the only fact that matters (the per-visit price *drops*), plus explicit permission to do nothing. The second half is not politeness — it is what keeps the sequence from feeling like pressure to someone who already buys from us.

## No VSL touch here

Deliberate. Existing customers do not need a prospect-facing explainer about why our pricing works — they have lived it. Sending it would read as condescending. See `campaigns/vsl-exact-price/distribution-map.md`.
