# Email 4: Honest Close

## Subject Line Variants

### A: "Yes, no, or a date to revisit" — recommended
Makes replying almost effortless by naming the three acceptable answers in the subject. Facility managers are busy and grateful for a message that respects that; this is the highest-reply-rate option.

### B: "Closing the file on this one"
Implies finality and prompts a response from anyone who does not want the option to disappear. Effective, but slightly transactional — use if A underperforms.

### C: "Should I follow up in the spring?"
Assumes the budget-cycle answer and asks permission to return. Softest option, and the best fit if the batch skews toward organizations on annual budgets (churches, practices).

**Recommended A/B test:** A vs C
**Reason:** Tests the direct three-option close against the budget-cycle assumption. If C wins, this audience is timing-constrained rather than undecided — which changes when we send the whole sequence.

## Email Copy

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

This is my last note about your cleaning quote. Three weeks feels like the right amount of follow-up, and past that it turns into pestering.

Any of these three is a genuinely useful answer:

**Yes** — reply and we will schedule a start date and send the certificate of insurance.

**No** — reply with one word. No explanation needed, and I will not ask for one. Knowing is better than wondering.

**Not yet** — tell me a month and I will follow up then, once, rather than checking in every few weeks in the meantime.

If it went to another company, that is a fine outcome. Facility cleaning is a real decision and there are other capable people in this market. My only suggestion is to get the checklist and the insurance certificate in writing before the first visit, whoever you choose.

The quote stands if you come back to it. There is no expiration and no pressure attached to it.
