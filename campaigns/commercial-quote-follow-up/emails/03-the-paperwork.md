# Email 3: The Paperwork

## Subject Line Variants

### A: "The paperwork, before you ask for it" — recommended
Signals that the friction is already handled — the single most welcome message you can send a facility manager mid-procurement. Anticipating the ask is itself the proof of competence.

### B: "COI, keys, and who you call"
Three concrete nouns, zero salesmanship. Reads like an internal note rather than marketing, which is exactly the register this stage wants.

### C: "12 questions worth asking any cleaning vendor"
Leads with the hiring sheet instead of the paperwork. Highest open potential because it is useful to someone still comparing — but it shifts the email's job from closing friction to educating. Use only if the sheet is attached.

**Recommended A/B test:** A vs C
**Reason:** Tests friction-removal against the lead magnet as the hook at the procurement stage. If C wins, the hiring sheet earns a permanent slot in commercial follow-up.

## Preview Text
"Certificate of insurance, access process, one point of contact."

## Send Timing
Day 10, Tuesday-Thursday 9:00 AM CT. By now the decision has usually moved from "which vendor" to "what do I need to get this approved."

## Email Copy

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

If the quote is still in play, here is the paperwork side, so you are not chasing it later.

**Insurance.** We carry $2 million in general liability. Reply and we will email the certificate of insurance today — most management companies and leases want it on file before the first visit, and it is easier to have it now than the week you are trying to start.

**Access.** Keys, codes, badges or a lockbox all work. Whatever you give us is logged and stored in a locked system, and only the team assigned to your building has it. If your access process needs to be documented for your own records, tell us what format you need.

**Who you call.** One number, and a person who knows your account. Not a ticket queue, and not a different regional office each time.

**After hours.** Most facilities prefer we work after close or before open. Tell us the window and we will schedule inside it.

One more thing that may be useful whether or not you choose us: we put together a one-page sheet of twelve questions worth asking any commercial cleaning company, with the answers to insist on in writing. It is a fair scorecard — we wrote it knowing it would be used on us too. Reply and I will send it.
